What is Day of China?

A small guide to Chinese traditional culture — taught the way it is lived, not the way it is listed in textbooks.

One day, not one encyclopedia

Each guide follows one day: Ba Duan Jin, Tai Chi, tea, calligraphy, cooking, Go, and the quiet habits in between — timed to the old twelve two-hour periods, 十二时辰 (shí'èr shíchén).

The first guide, A Day in the Life of a Chinese Person, is coming soon. The eleven moments are already on the site; the printable guides follow one moment at a time.

Who it's for

Curious beginners — English readers who know little about China and want to understand how ordinary Chinese people actually live a day. No background needed, no lectures, no lists of dynasties.

How the day works: the twelve two-hour periods

The old Chinese day is divided into twelve periods of two hours, each paired with an organ system in traditional medicine. The site's eleven moments are timed to it.

A note on accuracy

Everything on this site is public, checkable fact or clearly labelled tradition. Where we describe the old medicine — the twelve periods, the solar terms — we describe what the tradition says, not medical advice.
