10:30 · 巳时 · 9–11 · 脾经当令

The wet market

Picking vegetables by hand, bargaining a little, knowing the vendors by name.

What happens

By 10:30 the market is in full swing. Stalls of leafy greens, baskets of eggs, tanks of live fish, pork hanging from hooks. Vendors call out the day's prices; regulars stop at the same stalls they stopped at yesterday, and the vendors know them by name. Nothing is wrapped in plastic. You pick vegetables up with your hands, turn them over, and ask what's fresh today. The 菜市场 (càishìchǎng) is where dinner begins — not in a fridge, not in a delivery app.

Why this hour

The market run sits in 巳时 (sì shí), 9–11 a.m. — the spleen's hours in the old clock. In the traditional view, late morning belongs to digestion and nourishment: the body is turning breakfast into energy, and the day's food is bought now, fresh, so lunch can be cooked from it. Shopping daily, not weekly, is the point.

Try it

  1. Go before 11 — the fullest stalls, the freshest pick.
  2. Walk the whole market once before buying anything.
  3. Buy what's in season — ask the vendor “现在什么新鲜？” (What's fresh now?).
  4. Bargain lightly: ask the price, offer a little less, settle with a smile. Small amounts, small talk — not a battle.
  5. Buy for today only. Fresh, daily, nothing stored.

Words to know

菜市场 (càishìchǎng)
Wet market — the open fresh-food market.
讲价 (jiǎng jià)
To bargain, to haggle — lightly, with a smile.
新鲜 (xīn xiān)
Fresh — the first word to learn in any market.
摊主 (tān zhǔ)
Stall keeper — the person who knows your name by day three.

Questions

What is a wet market?

An open market selling fresh meat, fish, and vegetables. “Wet” because the floors are washed down through the day and the produce is fresh, not packaged.

Do vendors speak English?

Usually very little. Pointing, a calculator, and a smile go a long way — and the numbers are always shown, not just spoken.

How do I bargain without offending?

Ask the price first, then offer a little less and meet in the middle. Keep it light: small amounts, a smile, and accept the final price gracefully. Don't bargain hard over a few mao.

Cash or phone payment?

Phone payment (WeChat Pay or Alipay) is the norm in Chinese cities now. Cash still works, but small vendors may not have change for large notes.

When is the best time to go?

Early morning has the fullest stalls. 10:30 is when the day's shopping happens — late enough to be unhurried, early enough that nothing is picked over.
