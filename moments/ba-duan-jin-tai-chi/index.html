6:30 · 卯时 · 5–7 · 大肠经当令

Ba Duan Jin and Tai Chi in the park

The day starts slowly, in the park, moving with the neighbours.

What happens

Before the shops open and the traffic starts, the park is already full. Neighbours gather in loose groups on the paved clearings — some in their sixties and seventies, some younger, a few with grandchildren in tow. Nobody is in a hurry. The movements are slow and unshowy: arms rising, torsos turning, weight shifting from one foot to the other. This is 晨练 (chén liàn), the morning exercise — a daily habit for millions of Chinese people, and the first moment of the day.

Why this hour

The old day-clock divides the day into twelve two-hour periods, each paired with an organ system. 卯时 (mǎo shí), 5–7 a.m., belongs to the large intestine. In the traditional view, this is the hour when the body clears out the night — which is why the morning starts with movement and warm water, before breakfast. Whether or not you follow the old medicine, the logic is plain: move first, eat second.

Try it

  1. Wake before 7 and drink a cup of warm water.
  2. Find a park, a square, or any open ground near home.
  3. Do the Eight Brocades — eight gentle stretches: Two Hands Hold Up the Heavens; Draw the Bow to Shoot the Eagle; Raise One Arm to Regulate the Spleen and Stomach; Look Back to Cure the Five Strains and Seven Injuries; Shake the Head and Wag the Tail to Calm the Heart; Hold the Feet to Strengthen the Kidneys and Waist; Clench the Fists and Glare to Build Strength; Bounce on the Heels to Dispel Illness.
  4. Follow with a few minutes of Tai Chi: slow, flowing, breath and balance.
  5. Walk home and eat breakfast.

Words to know

八段锦 (bā duàn jǐn)
The Eight Brocades — eight gentle stretches.
太极 (tài jí)
Tai Chi — the Supreme Ultimate; slow, flowing movement.
卯时 (mǎo shí)
5–7 a.m. — the first two-hour period of the working day.
晨练 (chén liàn)
Morning exercise — the daily park habit.

Questions

What's the difference between Ba Duan Jin and Tai Chi?

Ba Duan Jin is a fixed set of eight stretches — qigong, or energy exercise. Tai Chi is a flowing sequence of connected movements, originally a martial art. Many people do both: stretches first to wake the body, then Tai Chi. Neither needs equipment.

Do I need a teacher to start?

A teacher helps with posture, but you can start from a video. Learn the eight stretches one at a time, slowly, and don't force any movement that hurts.

What time do people go?

Around 6:30, before breakfast. Parks fill up at dawn and empty out by 8, when the day's work starts.

Is it only for older people?

The dawn crowd in Chinese parks skews older, but all ages practise. Morning exercise is a habit, not an age group.

What should I wear?

Flat shoes and loose clothes. That's all.
