A Day in the Life of a Chinese Person

Learn Chinese culture the way it is actually lived — one ordinary day at a time. From morning tai chi in the park to a foot soak before bed, this is a day in the life of a Chinese person. Our first guide is on its way.

One day, ten moments

No lectures, no lists of dynasties. Just a day — the way millions of Chinese people still live it.

  1. 6:30

    Morning tai chi in the park

    The day starts slowly, in the park, moving with the neighbours. 太极 (tài jí) — tai chi.

  2. 7:30

    Breakfast

    Warm soy milk, a crisp 油条 (yóutiáo) fried dough stick, steamed 包子 (bāozi) buns, and a bowl of congee, 粥 (zhōu).

  3. 9:00

    Tea, reading, calligraphy

    A pot of tea, 茶 (chá), a book, and a brush. Calligraphy, 书法 (shūfǎ), is practice — not performance.

  4. 11:00

    The wet market

    Picking vegetables by hand, bargaining a little, knowing the vendors by name. The 菜市场 (càishìchǎng) is where dinner begins.

  5. 12:00

    Cook lunch, eat with family

    Two or three dishes, rice, and everyone at the table. Lunch is a family meal, not a desk meal.

  6. 13:00

    Nap

    A short midday rest, 午休 (wǔxiū). The whole country slows down for an hour.

  7. 15:00

    Afternoon tea, Go, chess, a stroll

    Another pot of tea, a game of Go, 围棋 (wéiqí), or Chinese chess, 象棋 (xiàngqí), and a slow walk around the block.

  8. 17:30

    Cook dinner, stew soup

    Dinner takes time. A soup, 煲汤 (bāotāng), simmers for hours — it is care, cooked slowly.

  9. 19:00

    Dinner, family chat

    The table fills up again. The day is talked over, dish by dish.

  10. 21:00

    Foot soak, early sleep

    Hot water, 泡脚 (pàojiǎo) — a foot soak — and an early night. Tomorrow starts at 6:30 again.

What is Day of China?

Day of China is a small guide to Chinese traditional culture — taught the way it is lived, not the way it is listed in textbooks. Each guide follows one day: tai chi, tea, calligraphy, cooking, Go, and the quiet habits in between.
