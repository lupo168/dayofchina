A Day in the Life of a Chinese Person

Learn Chinese culture the way it is actually lived — one ordinary day at a time. From morning Ba Duan Jin and Tai Chi in the park to a foot soak before bed, this is a day in the life of a Chinese person. Our first guide is on its way.

One day, eleven moments

No lectures, no lists of dynasties. Just a day — the way millions of Chinese people still live it, timed to the old twelve two-hour periods, from 卯时 at dawn to 亥时 at night.

  1. 6:30

    Ba Duan Jin and Tai Chi in the park

    卯时 · 5–7 · 大肠经当令

    The day starts slowly, in the park, moving with the neighbours. 八段锦 (bā duàn jǐn) — the Eight Brocades, eight gentle stretches to wake the body — followed by 太极 (tài jí), Tai Chi, slow and flowing, breath and balance.

  2. 7:30

    Make breakfast — noodles and dough, by hand

    辰时 · 7–9 · 胃经当令

    Breakfast is made, not bought. Hand-pulled noodles, 面食 (miànshí), steamed 包子 (bāozi) buns, warm soy milk, and a bowl of congee, 粥 (zhōu).

  3. 9:00

    Brew health tea, by hand

    巳时 · 9–11 · 脾经当令

    A pot of tea, 茶 (chá), blended for the season — a health tea, 养生茶 (yǎngshēng chá), made yourself. Then a book, and a brush: calligraphy, 书法 (shūfǎ), is practice — not performance.

  4. 10:30

    The wet market

    巳时 · 9–11 · 脾经当令

    Picking vegetables by hand, bargaining a little, knowing the vendors by name. The 菜市场 (càishìchǎng) is where dinner begins.

  5. 11:00

    Cook lunch — cold dishes and stir-fry

    午时 · 11–13 · 心经当令

    A cold dish, 凉菜 (liángcài), and a hot stir-fry, 炒菜 (chǎocài). Two or three dishes, cooked fresh.

  6. 12:00

    Lunch with family

    午时 · 11–13 · 心经当令

    Rice, two or three dishes, and everyone at the table. Lunch is a family meal, not a desk meal.

  7. 13:00

    Nap

    午时 · 11–13 · 子午觉

    A short midday rest, 午休 (wǔxiū) — the midday half of the old 子午觉, the two sleeps of the day. The whole country slows down for an hour.

  8. 15:00

    Afternoon tea, Go, chess, a stroll

    申时 · 15–17 · 膀胱经当令

    Another pot of tea, a game of Go, 围棋 (wéiqí), or Chinese chess, 象棋 (xiàngqí), and a slow walk around the block.

  9. 16:00

    Start the soup — seasonal, by the solar terms

    申时 · 15–17 · 膀胱经当令

    A soup, 煲汤 (bāotāng), simmers for hours — it is care, cooked slowly. The recipe follows the twenty-four solar terms, 二十四节气 (èrshísì jiéqì): what simmers in summer is not what simmers in winter.

  10. 18:30

    Dinner, family chat

    酉时 · 17–19 · 肾经当令

    The soup is on the table, the dishes are served. The table fills up again, and the day is talked over, dish by dish.

  11. 21:00

    Foot soak, early sleep

    亥时 · 21–23 · 三焦经当令

    Hot water, 泡脚 (pàojiǎo) — a foot soak — and an early night, asleep before 子时 (11 p.m.). Tomorrow starts at 6:30 again.

What is Day of China?

Day of China is a small guide to Chinese traditional culture — taught the way it is lived, not the way it is listed in textbooks. Each guide follows one day: Ba Duan Jin, Tai Chi, tea, calligraphy, cooking, Go, and the quiet habits in between — timed to the old twelve two-hour periods, 十二时辰.
